==============================================================================
CELL BLOCK ZERO
A prison break in the shape of the first Wolfenstein 3D, for LOVE 11.x
==============================================================================

Everything the game does, in one file. The README covers the same ground in
shorter form; this is the long version, written to be read while playing.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. RUNNING IT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  love .

from the game folder. To pack it into one file:

  zip -9 -r cellblockzero.love . -x '*.git*'
  love cellblockzero.love

It needs nothing else. Everything is drawn in code at startup: the walls and
the ground pixel by pixel, the guards, the pickups and the weapon in your
hands into a canvas, and every sound is synthesised. The art files under
assets/ are painted over that as the game loads, and the game runs the same
with that folder empty. assets/README.md says what goes in there and tools/
has the scripts that drew it.

The window opens fullscreen at the desktop resolution. F11 switches to a
window and back.

ON WINDOWS, install LOVE 11.5 from love2d.org, the 64 bit installer. Lua
itself is not needed, LOVE carries its own. Then either drag this folder onto
love.exe, or run it from a command prompt:

  "C:\Program Files\LOVE\love.exe" C:\Users\you\CellBlockZero

For a single file to double click, zip the contents of the folder, not the
folder itself, and rename the zip to cellblockzero.love. From PowerShell,
inside the folder:

  Compress-Archive -Path * -DestinationPath cellblockzero.zip
  Rename-Item cellblockzero.zip cellblockzero.love

Settings and the blocks you have opened live in
%APPDATA%\LOVE\prison-break-fps\progress.txt. Delete that file and
everything goes back to the way it comes out of the box.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. CONTROLS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Walk forward and back       W and S, or the up and down arrows
  Step sideways               A and D
  Turn                        the mouse, the left and right arrows, Q and E,
                              or hold alt with A and D
  Run                         shift
  Fire, or execute            left mouse button, or ctrl
  Open a door, use the exit   space
  Free a prisoner             space, standing in front of him
  Pick a weapon               1 to 7, or the mouse wheel
  Throw a grenade             G, or the right mouse button
  Throw a flash grenade       F
  Throw a knife               R, without putting your gun away
  Set a leg trap              B, on the floor in front of you
  Silencer on and off         T, or the middle mouse button
  Mouse speed                 [ and ]
  Map on and off              tab
  Look up and down            mouse, page up and page down, home to centre
  Pause                       P
  Controls on screen          F1
  Developer console           F2, or the tilde key
  Cheats                      F3 god, F4 invisible, F5 endless ammunition
  Mouse diagnostics           F6
  Fullscreen                  F11
  Back to the menu            escape, and the block waits for you there

Escape puts the block on hold and goes out to the menu. Nothing is lost: the
menu grows a line at the top, Carry on, which drops you back exactly where
you were, and escape again at the menu does the same. The clock on the end of
level screen stops while you are out there.

The pointer belongs to the game while a block is being played and comes back
when you pause, open the menu, open the console or leave the window.

WASD and the mouse split the work the way the games you already play do it:
A and D step sideways without changing where you face, and turning belongs to
the mouse. The keyboard still has a full set of turn keys for anyone playing
without a mouse, on the arrows, on Q and E, and on A and D while alt is held.

When a turn key and the mouse are used at the same time, the mouse has the
last word: while it is aiming, the turn keys stand down, and they take over
again a moment after the hand stops.

The menu works with the mouse as well as the keyboard: the line under the
pointer is the selected one, a click runs it, clicking a setting cycles it,
and the wheel moves the selection. A click also gets you past the briefing,
the death screen and the ending.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. SETTINGS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In the menu. All of them are kept in the save directory between sessions,
together with the blocks you have opened.

  Block          which block to start, limited to the ones you have opened
  Difficulty     Easy time, Hard time, Death row. See section 8
  Mouse input    automatic, or pointer with edge turns. See below
  Turn           normal, or reversed if your mouse turns you the wrong way
  Vertical look  normal, inverted, or off, which leaves the mouse turning
  Mouse speed    nine steps, the third by default

THE SIGHT SITS IN THE MIDDLE OF THE SCREEN and nothing moves it except you.
Walking rocks the view a little and firing kicks it, and neither touches the
sight: it stays where it is while the world moves around it. Push the mouse
up or down and the sight goes with it, which is the only thing that takes it
off centre. Let go and it comes back to the middle about a second later, so
it is never left pointing at the ceiling. Home puts it back at once.

Looking up and down is for seeing, not for aiming: a shot goes straight out
from the sight whatever the view is tilted to, and guards move with the
horizon, so they stay under the sight either way. Set Vertical look to off if
you would rather the mouse did nothing but turn, which is how this kind of
game was played in the first place.

IF TURNING GOES THE WRONG WAY, set Turn to reversed. The game itself turns the
way it should, this is checked by a test that casts rays and looks at what
lands where, so a wrong direction comes from the mouse or from the window
manager rather than from the game. F6 opens a panel that says which of the
three input paths is being used, whether the pointer can be recentred, what
the last movement reported, and how far you turned in the last second.

MOUSE INPUT. On automatic the game works out what it can use, in the order
below. If the pointer still ends up stuck against the edge of the window with
the view refusing to turn, set this to pointer: the game then never asks for
relative mode at all, drives the view from the pointer alone, hides the cursor
with an empty cursor image, and turns on its own while the pointer sits
against an edge. It costs nothing on a machine where the usual way works, so
it is a safe thing to leave on.

THE THREE PATHS. Normally the game asks for relative mouse mode, the pointer
is parked and only the movement counts, which is what every shooter does.
Some window managers say yes to that and then do nothing about it, so the game
does not take their word for it: if the pointer is seen to move, relative mode
is treated as absent however it answers. Then the turn comes from the motion
events when they arrive, or from reading the pointer twice when they do not,
never from both, and the pointer is dragged back to the middle of the window
whenever it strays. The drag happens early, well before the pointer can reach a
side, and if the pointer slips out of the window it is taken back as soon as
that is noticed. Where the drag is refused the pointer ends up parked against
the edge with nowhere left to go, and the view keeps turning on its own for as
long as the hand is still moving, stopping a quarter of a second after the
mouse is let go so a pointer left out there cannot spin you round and round.

Under WSL the arrow you see may be the Windows pointer rather than the one the
game controls, and hiding that is not something the game can reach. Keeping
the pointer dragged back to the middle is the next best thing: it sits under
the crosshair instead of wandering off.

F6 tells you which of these you are in: what relative mode claims, whether the
pointer is seen moving, which path the last turn came through, and whether the
recentre takes. The cursor is hidden twice over, by asking and by putting an
empty cursor on, because asking is also ignored in places. Running in a window
with F11 instead of fullscreen is worth a try: some window managers hand the
pointer over properly in one mode and not in the other.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE POINT OF IT
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

You are twenty years into a sentence. The guard who let himself into your
cell an hour after lights out was not there to talk. He is on the floor now
and you are wearing what he brought with him: a hatchet, a revolver, twenty
bullets and a vest, torn in the scuffle and half as good as it was. The sun
comes up in a few hours and you intend to be a long way from here.

Out through three blocks:

  1. D Block, the cells, the wings and the stairwell
  2. Laundry and workshops, the service floor
  3. The yard and the gatehouse, open ground and the riot squad

Each block ends at an exit tile, a door or a gate you press space at. Walking
out of a block opens the next one in the menu, and nothing else does: you
cannot pick a block you have not earned. Health, armour, weapons and
ammunition carry from one block into the next.

Dying puts you back at the start of the block you were in, with the loadout
you walked in with. The block restarts completely, so the bodies and the
dropped weapons from your last attempt are gone.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WEAPONS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Slot 1  Hatchet          22 to 34 damage, reaches 1.5 tiles, no ammunition
                           Twice as hard when you hit a guard from behind.
                           Carries 2.5 tiles of noise, which is nothing.

  Slot 2  Revolver         17 to 27 damage, reaches 28 tiles, one shot per
                           press, bullets. Carries 8 tiles of noise, 4 with
                           the silencer on.

  Slot 3  Uzi              6 to 11 damage a round, eleven rounds a second
                           while you hold the button, reaches 22 tiles,
                           bullets. Carries 7 tiles of noise, 5 with the
                           silencer on. Spreads more the faster you move.

  Slot 4  Rocket launcher  90 damage at the centre of a blast 2.7 tiles
                           across, rockets. It hurts you too. Carries 12
                           tiles of noise, and the explosion itself 14.

  Slot 5  Grenade          72 damage over 2.5 tiles, a fuse of 1.6 seconds.
                           It arcs, bounces off walls and rolls, so it can be
                           put round a corner. The throw itself is quiet.

  Slot 6  Flash grenade    no damage. Everything that can see it within 6.5
                           tiles is blinded for up to 4.5 seconds, you
                           included if you are close and looking. A blinded
                           guard stops shooting and backs away.

  Slot 7  Throwing knife   press 7 to hold it and fire to throw, or press R
                           at any time and it is thrown without putting the
                           gun away. 45 damage, 77 in the back, where it
                           kills a guard
                           or a sergeant outright and leaves a riot captain
                           on his last legs. Carries 2 tiles of noise, which
                           is less than the body makes. Three throws in a
                           blade: it lands on the floor where it hit, you
                           walk over it to pick it up again, and the third
                           throw snaps it. You can carry four blades.

  Not a slot  Leg trap     B sets one on the floor in front of you, and it
                           stays there until something walks into it. 60 to a
                           dog, which kills it outright, 45 to a man, which
                           kills a guard and leaves anybody bigger pinned for
                           two and a half seconds and shouting about it. You
                           never spring your own. Carry up to four.

AIMING. The sight sits on the horizon, and that is exactly where the bullet
goes. A shot connects only while the sight is on the figure itself, roughly
half a tile across the shoulders, so a round that passes beside a guard misses
him however close it looked. The uzi spreads a little, and more the faster you
are moving, which is the one thing that puts a round somewhere you did not
point it.

The vertical look is for seeing and not for aiming: guards move up and down
the screen with the horizon, so they stay under the sight whatever the view
is doing. The sight itself only moves when you move it, and comes back to the
middle about a second after you stop. Home levels it at once.

The view dips a little with each step and kicks when you fire. Neither of
those moves the sight: the world rocks, the sight does not.

The revolver and the uzi share one pool of bullets.

Carrying limits: 150 bullets, 20 rockets, 9 grenades, 6 flash grenades.

THE SILENCER is a pickup. Once you have it, T fits it to the revolver or the
uzi and takes it off again. A fitted can costs fifteen percent of the damage
and most of the noise, and it is the difference between killing one guard and
fighting the whole wing. The status bar says "+ can" when it is on.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. HEALTH, VEST AND PICKUPS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Health starts at 100 and stops there. The vest starts at 50 percent and goes
to 100. While you have any vest left it soaks 55 percent of every hit and
wears down by what it soaked, so it runs out faster in a long fight.

  Uzi                 the weapon, and 16 bullets
  Rocket launcher     the weapon, and 3 rockets
  Grenades            2 grenades
  Flash grenades      2 flash grenades
  Bullets             8 bullets
  Box of bullets      25 bullets
  Rockets             2 rockets
  Vest                25 percent
  Riot vest           50 percent
  First aid           25 health
  Cold food           10 health
  Silencer            fits the revolver and the uzi
  Red, blue, yellow key   opens the door of that colour, in that block only

You walk over a pickup to take it, and you leave it where it is if you are
already full of what it holds, so nothing is wasted.

Most guards are carrying nothing worth taking. Roughly one in three of the
blue ones leaves a handful of bullets, and about half of the sergeants and the
captains leave something, which for a captain might be rockets, grenades or a
vest. What they drop stays on the floor. Across a whole block that works out
at four or five bodies worth picking over out of ten or more, so ammunition is
something to spend carefully rather than something to swim in.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. QUIET WORK
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

EXECUTIONS. Walk up to a guard who has not seen you, or stand behind one who
has lost you, and the fire button kills him in one move instead of shooting.
You need to be within about a tile and facing him. With the hatchet in hand it
is the throat or the back of the head, bare handed it is the neck. All three
are silent, so the guard at the end of the corridor keeps walking his round.
The screen tells you when it is on offer.

THE THROWING KNIFE is the other quiet way of doing it, and the only one that
works at a distance. It drops where it hit, so a kill at the end of a
corridor means walking down there to get the blade back. Three throws and it
is scrap.

GUARDS ASLEEP IN A CHAIR turn up here and there, usually by a door that gets
little traffic. A sleeper sees nothing and hears nothing until a noise wakes
him, so he can be walked up to from the front and executed like any guard
caught unaware. Anything louder than a knife will wake him: a shot nearby, a
door being opened next to him, a shout, and the klaxon wakes every sleeper in
the block. Once up he is an ordinary guard of his tier, groggy for about a
second.

LEG TRAPS are for the two things you cannot walk up behind. A dog comes at
you in a straight line and does not look down, so a trap in the doorway it
will come through kills it before it reaches you. A guard walking his round is
doing the same thing, and a trap on his route takes him out of the block
without a shot. An alert guard is different: he is looking where he is going
and spots a trap about seven times in ten, and then he steps over it.

Measured, somebody sent straight down a lane with a trap in it, twenty runs
of each:

  a dog, either way                 sprang it 20 of 20, died 20 of 20
  a guard on his round              sprang it 20 of 20, died 20 of 20
  a guard already hunting you       sprang it  6 of 20
  a riot captain already hunting    sprang it  5 of 20, lived, pinned and
                                    down about half his health

Traps come out of lockers, and there are one or two lying about each block.
Setting one makes almost no noise; what happens afterwards does.

BODIES stay where they fall for the rest of the block. A guard who walks past
one raises the alarm and starts looking, so where you leave them matters.

NOISE is what gives you away more often than your silhouette. Every shot
carries the distance listed in section 5, measured on foot through the
doorways rather than straight through walls, and it is answered by the two to
four guards nearest to it, never by the whole block. Anyone further off who
heard something only gets sharper where he stands.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
8. THE OTHER PRISONERS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Some cells are not empty. Most of what is in them is scenery and says what
this place is: a skeleton still hanging from the shackles on the wall, a man
who used his own sheet and the stool he kicked over, a body face down, and in
one or two of them nothing anybody could put a name to. None of it moves and
none of it can be used.

Some of them are alive. Open his cell door with space the way you open any
door, step in front of him, and the screen offers "[space] tell him to come
with you". Press it and he is gone: he does not wait for you and he does not
tag along. He goes looking for guards, the nearest one first, wherever in the
block that is, working any door that is not locked with a key, bars included.
When he finds one he goes at him with his hands. He only falls in behind you
when there is nobody left on the level to go after.

Let two out in D Block and they take about three guards down between them
before they are both dead, which takes under a minute. That is what they are:
a minute of somebody else's problem.

What he is worth, measured over forty fights of each kind:

  one on one with a guard, three tiles apart     he wins about three in four
  the same fight starting seven tiles apart      he dies about three in four
  one on one with a sergeant                     he dies, almost every time
  coming at a guard who has not noticed him      he wins every time
  coming at a sergeant from behind               he wins about two in three
  coming at a riot captain from behind           he dies, having taken half
                                                 the captain's health with him

So he is not a soldier. He has sixteen hit points, no gun and no armour, and
a guard who sees him coming across open ground kills him before he arrives.
Used properly he is a distraction that hits back: while a guard is dealing
with him, that guard is not dealing with you.

Two other things worth knowing. A guard who has lost you will fight a loose
prisoner where he stands, and seeing one sets off the klaxon, but it tells
nobody where you are. And your bullets do not pass through him: a prisoner
between you and a guard takes the shot.

The status bar counts them: how many are out, and how many are still on
their feet.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
9. THE GUARDS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Three tiers, one brain, told apart by the uniform.

                        Guard         Sergeant      Riot captain
  uniform               blue          olive, vest   black, red visor
  health                28            48            85
  speed                 1.95          2.35          2.7 tiles a second
  eyesight              15 tiles      18 tiles      22 tiles
  hearing               8 tiles       10 tiles      12 tiles
  reaction              0.4 to 0.7s   0.22 to 0.4s  0.12 to 0.24s
  damage a shot         5 to 10       4 to 8        9 to 15
  shots in a burst      1 to 2        3 to 5        2 to 3
  keeps a distance of   5.5 tiles     7 tiles       4.5 tiles
  gives up after        11 seconds    20 seconds    40 seconds
  throws grenades       no            rarely        often

THE DOGS are the fourth kind and do not count as guards. They carry nothing,
tell nobody anything and have no round to keep: they roam their end of the
block and run at the nearest prisoner they can see, which means you or any
man you have let out.

                        Dog
  health                34
  speed                 3.5 tiles a second, faster than anybody
  eyesight              13 tiles
  hearing               12 tiles
  damage a bite         11 to 19, about twice a second, within a tile

A dog beats a freed prisoner about three times in four, which is what they
are for. Against you they are two revolver rounds, as long as you see one
coming: the bark is the warning.

THEY ONLY OPEN FIRE WHEN THEY CAN SEE YOU. Nothing else makes a guard shoot.

ON A QUIET ROUND each of them is doing one of three things, picked when the
block starts so two in the same corridor do not move alike: standing a post
and scanning from it, pacing a route and walking it back, or sweeping a wing
by picking a new corner of it each time he arrives. They stop to look around,
and they keep to the corridors and the rooms: on a quiet round nobody rattles
cell doors, which is the only reason you get out of yours.

A SHOT IS ONLY HEARD IN THE ROOM IT WAS FIRED IN. The block is cut into
areas, a room or a stretch of corridor between doors, and gunfire carries
inside one of them and no further. Anybody in the next room along hears
something, gets sharper and walks a different round, but he is not coming and
he has no idea where you are. Rockets and grenades are the exception: a blast
goes through the whole wing, everyone hears it, and the two or three nearest
to it walk in while the rest change what they are doing where they stand.

NOBODY LEAVES HIS CORNER EMPTY. A guard belongs to the area he starts in and
his round stays inside it. He will walk out of it to go and look at something,
but only while a colleague is still in there: the last man in a room holds it
and watches the door instead of going.

WHEN SOMETHING HAPPENS they go and look, which is not the same as attacking. A
shot, a scream, a door or a body sends the nearest two to four to the spot.
Each of them walks to the place he has a reason to believe you are, on a path
worked out to that spot, and when he arrives and finds nothing he looks around
and tries the next corner along until he runs out of patience. Nobody ever
walks towards where you actually are. Break line of sight and move, and what
follows you is a search.

THEY START SPREAD OUT. However a level file is drawn, nobody begins the block
standing on top of a colleague: anyone who starts crowded is moved to a free
tile of his own area, so you never walk into four men in one doorway.

THE KLAXON goes off the first time anyone sees you. It reaches the whole block
and makes every guard sharper for a few seconds, a wider cone, more scanning,
quicker to react, but it tells nobody where you are. The guard who saw you
calls it in on the radio, and that reaches five and a half tiles and two
colleagues at a time.

IN A FIGHT they hold the distance their weapon likes, strafe while they shoot,
spread out instead of queueing in the doorway, open doors, step out of the way
of a rocket, break line of sight to recover when they are hurting, and lob a
grenade at the corner they last saw you behind. They are solid to each other,
so a squad arrives spread across the room instead of stacked in one tile.

DIFFICULTY changes four things: the damage they do, how often they hit, how
long they take to react, and how many of them answer one noise.

  Easy time    65 percent damage, 78 percent accuracy, slow reaction, 2 answer
  Hard time    the numbers in the table above, 3 answer
  Death row    140 percent damage, 122 percent accuracy, quick off the
               mark, 4 answer

However loud it gets, only that many guards are sent anywhere, and the number
on their feet at any moment is capped at one more than that. A guard who sees
you with his own eyes always engages, cap or no cap, so walking into a room
full of them is still a bad idea, but a shot in a corridor brings three or
four, never the block.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
10. DOORS, KEYS AND THE MAP
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Press space in front of a door to open it, and in front of an exit tile to
finish the block. A door slides into the wall it is flush with and closes
again on its own a few seconds later unless somebody is standing in it.

Three locks, red, blue and yellow, each needing the key of that colour. A key
is never lying on the floor waiting for you. Each one is in one of three
places, and the level file says which:

  on a guard     the man furthest from where you start is carrying it, and
                 he drops it when he goes down, whichever way he goes down
  in a locker    the locker furthest from where you start has it among
                 whatever else is in there
  behind a panel a section of wall somewhere slides back, and it is in the
                 space behind it

Keys do not carry from one block to the next.

LOCKERS are the steel cupboards set into the walls. Press space in front of
one and it opens, once. What is in it was decided when the block loaded: it
can be bullets, a box of them, food, a first aid kit, a vest, a leg trap,
grenades, flash grenades, a knife, rockets, an uzi, a riot vest, the
silencer, two or three of those, or nothing at all. Every block has armour in
a locker somewhere: if the dice leave it out, one is put in. Roughly one in
five has already been cleared out. Whatever is in there falls at your feet,
so you pick it up by walking into it.

PANELS are sections of wall that slide back when you press space on them.
They are built out of the same wall as everything around them, so from down
the corridor there is nothing to see, but one has been pushed before and it
shows: a hairline down both sides where it does not quite meet the rest, the
dust and the scrape marks along the floor at its foot, and a worn patch at
chest height where hands have pushed it. Walk up to one and the screen says
"[space] this section is loose". They never show on the map. They are worth
looking for because that is where a key or a cache is.

Tab turns the map on and off. It sits in a corner and shows the walls, the
doors coloured by their lock, the exit in green, the pickups, the guards in
orange while they are calm and red once they are not, the bodies in dark red,
and you as an arrow.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
11. TESTING IT: CONSOLE, CHEATS, SHOOTING RANGE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The developer console is on F2 or on the tilde key. It pauses the game, takes
a line and runs it. Up and down recall what you typed before, escape closes
it, and "help" lists everything.

  god                 no damage, also on F3
  invis               the guards cannot see you, also on F4
  ammo                ammunition never runs out, also on F5
  noclip              walk through walls
  give <what>         give all, or weapons, ammo, keys, or one weapon by name
  heal [amount]       default full
  armor [amount]      default full
  guard <tier> [n] [dummy|asleep]
                      drop n guards of that tier in front of you, tier 4
                      being a dog. "dummy" makes them stand there and take it
                      without fighting back, "asleep" puts them in a chair
  dog [n]             the same as guard 4
  trap [n]            put n leg traps in your pocket, four by default
  clear               remove every guard
  killall             kill every guard where he stands
  distract            every guard forgets you and goes back to his round
  free                let every prisoner on the level out of his cell
  mate                put one freed prisoner in front of you
  freeze              guards stop thinking, on and off
  dmg                 print what every one of your hits took off
  where               print your position, your tile and your facing
  tp <x> <y>          move yourself there
  block <1..3>        load a block, ignoring what you have opened
  range               the shooting range
  restart             start this level again

Whatever cheat is on is printed in green above the status bar. F6 shows the
mouse diagnostics described in section 3.

THE SHOOTING RANGE, from the menu or with "range", is a long hall with a bench
holding every weapon, every box of ammunition, both vests and the silencer.
Targets stand down the lane at roughly 5, 15, 25, 35 and 40 tiles, with three
more standing together for testing a blast. The markers on the walls are five
tiles apart. The targets do not move and do not shoot back, they say what each
hit took off them, and they get back up a couple of seconds after they drop,
so you can measure a weapon and see exactly where its range runs out. The two
furthest targets are past every barrel in the game, which is the point of
them.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. LEVEL FILES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

A level is a text file in levels/. Everything above the [map] marker is a
"key = value" header, everything below it is the grid, read exactly as typed:
a space and a dot are both floor, so trailing blanks count. Lines starting
with a semicolon are comments.

  name      shown on the loading screen and the status bar
  next      the file to load after this one. Leaving it out ends the game
  brief     the paragraph on the loading screen
  ceiling   three numbers from 0 to 1, the ceiling colour
  floor     the floor colour
  redkey    where the red key is: guard, locker or secret. Leaving it out
            means there is no red key in this block
  bluekey   the same for the blue one
  yellowkey the same for the yellow one
  dummies   true makes every guard a target that will not fight back
  respawn   true brings dropped targets back
  readout   true prints the damage of every hit

Tiles:

  WALLS     #  stone        =  brick       %  cell tile
            &  metal        ~  rock        *  wall with a poster

  DOORS     |  steel        +  barred cell door
            r  locked red   b  locked blue   y  locked yellow

  EXIT      X  a wall tile you press space at to finish the block

  START     N S W E, one per level, the letter is the way you face

  GUARDS    1  guard        2  sergeant    3  riot captain
            4  dog          z  a guard asleep in a chair
            Z  a sergeant asleep in a chair

  WEAPONS   u  uzi          l  rocket launcher
            g  grenades     f  flash grenades
            K  throwing knife, three throws in it

  AMMO      a  bullets      A  box of bullets      o  rockets

  GEAR      v  vest         V  riot vest   m  first aid
            h  food         s  silencer

  KEYS      R  red          B  blue        Y  yellow

  TRAP      T  a leg trap to carry, set with B

  LOCKER    L  a steel cupboard set into the wall. Solid, opens once

  PANEL     /  a section of wall that slides back when pushed. It is
               drawn as whatever its neighbours are

  SCENERY   k  a skeleton chained to the wall, sitting where he died
            n  a prisoner who hanged himself, and the stool he kicked
            d  a dead prisoner face down on the floor
            c  what is left of one, and the blood around it

  PRISONER  p  a prisoner who is still alive. Open his door, stand in
               front of him and press space, and he fights for you

A door slides into the wall it is flush with, so a door wants solid tiles on
both sides along one axis. Put a locked door in a wall and put its key
somewhere reachable without it.

To add a block: write levels/04.txt, point the "next" of 03.txt at it, and add
its path to the LEVELS list at the top of main.lua.

An example, the smallest level that works:

  name = Test
  brief = one room, one guard, one door out

  [map]
  ##########
  #........#
  #.E....1.#
  #........#
  #####X####
  [end]

13. WHAT IS IN WHICH FILE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  main.lua              love callbacks, menu, the run through the blocks
  conf.lua              window settings
  levels/               the level files, plain text
  src/level.lua         reads a level file, doors, line of sight, ray casting
  src/raycaster.lua     the 3D view: walls, sprites, the depth buffer
  src/textures.lua      wall, door and ground textures, pixel by pixel
  assets/               art painted over what the code draws
  tools/                the scripts that drew what is in assets/
  src/art.lua           guards, pickups, explosions, the weapon in your hands
  src/world.lua         one level being played, and everything in it
  src/player.lua        movement, weapons, pickups, the executions
  src/weapons.lua       what the player can carry
  src/enemy.lua         the guards: senses, rounds, fighting, dying
  src/nav.lua           the shared distance field, paths, cover and flanking
  src/projectile.lua    rockets and grenades
  src/fx.lua            blood, sparks, smoke, fireballs
  src/hud.lua           status bar, map, menus
  src/audio.lua         generated sound effects
  src/progress.lua      which blocks are open, and the settings
  src/console.lua       the developer console and the cheats
